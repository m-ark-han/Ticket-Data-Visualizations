3. Destination field dictionary proposal

The county ticket field is named DisposalSite, but a pickup ticket's label says only the destination entered on that record. It does not by itself establish receipt, recycling, processing, or final disposal. Keep the original label and store a separate, versioned destination_type with a source and confidence level. EPA's debris-planning guidance distinguishes temporary staging and processing from later recycling or final disposal.

Standard categories: temporary debris management site (staging, sorting, or reduction); recycling or material-recovery facility; permitted landfill / final disposal; transfer or treatment facility; other named receiving site; unknown / blank. A facility may perform multiple activities, so retain its primary classification, accepted material streams, active dates, evidence URL, and verification status. Do not treat “FDS” or a site name as proof of a particular load outcome. No Pinellas label is classified as a confirmed landfill from this ticket field alone. 5. Why these figures belong in a report or system

  • Weekly volume (F05) and cumulative milestones: Pearland's removal-per-day chart and Lee County's dated totals show why pace and accumulated work belong together. They require dated, consistent volume records; ticket gaps must remain visible.
  • Zone timing map (F21): League City and Lee County locate work by area or road pass. Our map answers a narrower ticket-based timing question; verified pass or clearance status requires an operations feed.
  • Debris-type share (F11): Pearland and Lee County separate material streams. Compare both ticket share and CY share because loads differ in size.
  • Destination-label figure (F22) and dictionary: Louisiana's approved-site table, EPA's facility map, and North Carolina's active-site map show why type, allowed activity, and current status are separate fields. Keep label totals and site classification distinct until unload and haulout records can establish a route.
  • Data-quality map (F25) and repeated-address map (F26): reliable location data is a prerequisite for neighborhood reporting. These figures identify records to inspect, not confirmed errors or cleanup outcomes.
  • Initial versus remaining estimates: Pearland and Pinellas display volume-based progress. Use a percentage only with dated, comparable estimates and a defined completion measure.

6. Reading the repeated-address figure

Figure F26 includes address labels that appear on at least ten pickup tickets. A circle sits at the median recorded pickup coordinate for a label. Its size represents the number of tickets; its color represents how widely that label's ticket coordinates are spread (90th-percentile distance from the median). Fifteen labels exceed 1 kilometer on that spread measure. The map helps identify broad or ambiguous labels for review. A larger circle does not mean more cubic yards: this visualization does not sum volume by address. Road, park and staging-area labels may legitimately cover more than one pickup point.
