St. Petersburg retrospective: three additional figures

These figures use the separate St. Petersburg debris-ticket layer reviewed in the report draft. They cover 6,694 records dated October 3–23, 2024. The neighborhood figure also uses the city's Neighborhood Associations layer. They are separate from the nine Pinellas County figures on the main page.

2. Steady or intermittent collection by neighborhood?

Each colored square marks a date with an ordinary construction-and-demolition or vegetative collection ticket. Darker colors mean more tickets on that date; pale squares mean no matching ticket. Dates use local TicketDate. The first-to-last span is an observed ticket window, not verified cleanup duration.

Of 1,923 eligible tickets, 1,743 matched one city association polygon and 180 matched none. The current boundaries may differ from 2024 boundaries. A pale square means no matched ticket in this snapshot, not no service.

3. How complete are receiving-site labels?

Each bar shows the share of an activity group's tickets carrying each original DisposalSite label. Hover or tap for the exact ticket count and share. “MISSING” is a literal value in the public layer, not an empty cell. A named receiving site does not establish final disposal or recycling. What else is recorded on those tickets?

All 3,601 tickets labeled MISSING use special-service format 3. Their DisposalSiteGPS is also literally MISSING. HazardClass and SvcCode identify the service, while SvcName says “CITY OF ST. PETERSBURG” on all 3,601; those fields do not name a receiving facility. Notes has text on 604 of these tickets, but the public fields checked do not yield a reliable replacement site label.

For these 3,601 records, Volume equals UnitCount on every ticket, Weight is zero, and ScaleTicketNumber is “N/A.” That pattern suggests service-unit records rather than ordinary measured debris loads; the city's field definitions are needed to confirm the workflow. Do not treat these values as cubic yards or infer a destination from pickup GPS or address.

The MISSING labels cover 2,028 hanger, 798 electronic-scrap, 712 white-goods, 35 household hazardous-waste, 17 freon-removal, nine leaner, and two “N/A” service tickets. The other six hazardous-waste tickets have named site labels. The public service has a ticket layer and a project-boundary layer, but no linked unload or haulout table. A separate city operations record may still exist.
