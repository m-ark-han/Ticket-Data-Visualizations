St. Petersburg retrospective: three additional figures

These figures use the separate St. Petersburg debris-ticket layer reviewed in the report draft. They cover 6,694 records dated October 3–23, 2024. The neighborhood figure also uses the city's Neighborhood Associations layer. They are separate from the nine Pinellas County figures on the main page.

1. What drove each day's ticket count?

Daily ticket counts by activity group, based on the local date of LoadDate. Each ticket appears once; household hazardous waste is separated from other special services. Empty dates remain on the axis. Hover or tap for exact counts, and drag to zoom.

This chart counts tickets, not comparable volumes or operating hours. No records on a date do not prove crews were idle.

2. Steady or intermittent collection by neighborhood?

Each colored square marks a date with an ordinary construction-and-demolition or vegetative collection ticket. Darker colors mean more tickets on that date; pale squares mean no matching ticket. Dates use local TicketDate. The first-to-last span is an observed ticket window, not verified cleanup duration.

Of 1,923 eligible tickets, 1,743 matched one city association polygon and 180 matched none. The current boundaries may differ from 2024 boundaries. A pale square means no matched ticket in this snapshot, not no service.

3. How complete are receiving-site labels?

Each bar shows the share of an activity group's tickets carrying each original DisposalSite label. Hover or tap for the exact ticket count and share. “MISSING” means this column is blank; the city has not yet confirmed whether another field records a destination for special services. A named receiving site does not establish final disposal or recycling. Read Mark's field review.
