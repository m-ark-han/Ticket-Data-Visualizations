Receiving-site label investigation

In the St. Petersburg snapshot, DisposalSite is blank for 3,601 tickets. The blank values are concentrated in records classified by DocumentFormatID as special services: all 3,566 “other special services” and 35 of 41 household hazardous-waste tickets. The other six hazardous-waste tickets show three labels for 7TH AND 22ND DMS and three for NXGEN ENVIRONMENTAL. Ordinary collection and citizen drop-off have no blank DisposalSite values in this snapshot.

HazardClass and DebrisType explain the activity grouping; TicketDate, LoadDate, and Volume do not identify a receiving site. The extracted analysis fields contain no alternate verified receiving-site value. Conclusion: report “blank in the DisposalSite column,” not “destination unknown.” Check the complete city schema, service-specific fields, unload records, and city column definitions before making a stronger claim. The Pinellas County destination labels come from a different dataset and cannot fill these blanks.

Neighborhood timing and clearance definition

  1. Observed activity: for each neighborhood, show first ticket date, last ticket date, elapsed days between them, count of dates with tickets, longest internal no-ticket gap, ticket count, and matched-ticket coverage. These can be calculated from the static St. Petersburg snapshot.
  2. 90% of recorded volume: if per-ticket CY and neighborhood matching are validated, find the first date cumulative recorded CY reaches 90% of that neighborhood's snapshot total. Label the result “days to 90% of recorded CY.” F21 already uses this idea for county debris zones, not St. Petersburg neighborhoods.
  3. Verified clearance: show an ongoing/completed status and clearance date only when an agency work-area status, final-pass record, or signed inspection supplies them. Keep the authority, status definition, update date, and boundary version. A last ticket or a 90% volume date cannot supply that status.
  4. Estimate-based progress: initial and remaining debris estimates may show a possible backlog only after units, area boundaries, debris types, methods, and timestamps are reconciled. Do not use 1 − remaining ÷ initial as a completion percentage without that validation.

The St. Petersburg layer used for the current neighborhood calendar is a static October 3–23, 2024 snapshot. To describe current conditions, use a live authoritative status API with a visible refresh time. To describe a finished operation retrospectively, use the final status history and its approval or inspection date.

Dashboard patterns reviewed

The Pinellas County dashboard originals use daily load volume, a separate sand view, initial/remaining estimate charts, and a work-area status map. Esri's Emergency Debris Management workflow also describes status by work area, total volume removed, volume by debris type and provider, separate haulout/final-disposal charts, and ticket lists. These examples support a compact dashboard that distinguishes collection, operational status, estimates, and later disposal stages.
